# fp `5ix:BuyExactSolIn cu_price=75210`: entry selection is empty, one exit level pays 3.5pp, and the cohort is dying

Self-run sweep over the 292 launches of `cu_limit=301000 / cu_price=75210` (07-28 .. 08-17),
answering whether a metric combo beats the blind `held >= 60` hold. Fills are next-slot
(`first` / `median` / `worst`), costs 125 bps/leg + 0.000225 SOL/leg + `B/vsol` impact at
0.126 SOL. Companion to [maxbuy-launcher-fingerprint.md](../plans/strategies/maxbuy-launcher-fingerprint.md).

## The operator's habit

One creator wallet **per launch** — 292 launches, 292 distinct creators, so funding-graph
tracing is closed and the creator is only identifiable as the `Create_v2` signer of that mint.
Dev buy 15.06 SOL, entry `vsol` 44.0 (p25 41.6 / p75 49.4), ATH `vsol` 65.4. He sells on
265 of 292 and takes out a median **1.38x** what he put in.

**His first sell lands a median +3.1 s AFTER the ATH** (p10 0.0, p25 +0.6, p75 +12.1). He does
not distribute ahead of the peak — his selling *is* the peak. From ATH the collapse to -50%
takes a median of **4.2 s / 10 slots** (p25 1.2 s / 3 slots), and -30% and -50% arrive at the
same moment: once it goes it is over in one move.

## Reacting to his dump is the worst rule measured

Exit on the creator's first sell of any size: **-40.20%/trade, 10.7% win, 1 of 21 days
positive**. Raising the trigger to 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 5 / 10 SOL changes nothing (-40.2 to -41.3).
The rule cannot work in principle — the trigger fires at the top and the fill lands after the
cascade. **Every "sell just before the rug" shape on this cohort is refuted by this one row.**

## Entry selection is empty, in both forms

Entry gates that *wait* for confirmation lose money monotonically on five unrelated axes
(`cum_org_net`, `orgbuy_5s`, `orgbuy_10s`, `vsol`, `n_prints`) — e.g. `cum_org_net >= 1/2/3/5/8`
runs +17.2 / +13.8 / +10.7 / +5.4 / -0.0 against +20.8 ungated. Price is `vsol^2` against a
fixed ceiling, so every second of confirmation is bought with headroom.

Entry *screens* — judge at a fixed time, admit or reject, no delay — hold per-trade flat while
the book halves: `cum_org_net >= 1 @3s` books +20.94 on n=138 against +20.75 on n=291, and
total SOL falls 7.61 -> 3.64. That is the signature of removing a random half.

**Earlier is strictly better**: entering at 0 s books +22.78 at `worst_case` against +19.86 at
3 s and +6.69 at 20 s. The cohort has one launch template and does not vary; there is nothing
to select on.

## The one thing that pays: his payoff level, not his action

`cum_org_net` — cumulative non-creator net SOL inflow — is his decision variable, and a
*level* on it is persistent state, so it survives the fill ladder.

| exit | first | median | **worst** | worst median |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `held >= 60` | +18.96 | +17.29 | **+16.23** | +29.28 |
| `held >= 120` | +11.88 | +11.02 | +10.30 | +23.88 |
| **`cum_org_net >= 10` OR `held >= 120`** | +21.15 | +20.75 | **+19.86** | +30.50 |
| `cum_org_net >= 12` OR `held >= 120` | +22.56 | +21.04 | +19.87 | +36.00 |

Against a **matched-hold control** (`held >= 54`, the champion's own median hold) it wins
+3.35pp of mean and +5.8pp of median, and it repeats in both halves at `worst_case`:
IS +24.58 vs +20.54, OOS +11.13 vs +8.25.

Two limits on believing it. It **fails the tie-break gate** — 20 random same-size exit draws
mean +14.93 sd 4.60, putting the champion at **z = 1.26**, inside noise. And the `>= 12`
variant is better IS (+27.60) and worse OOS (+5.55) than `>= 10`, which is what a fitted
threshold looks like. Treat 10 as the robust member of a shallow ridge, not as a tuned value.

## Partial exits cost 5.7pp

Bank 50% at `cum_org_net >= X`, remainder to `held >= 120`, against the un-laddered champion:

| ladder | mean | median | win |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| control, no ladder | **+20.75** | **+31.49** | **81.1%** |
| bank 50% @ `org >= 10` | +15.02 | +22.53 | 57.4% |
| bank 50% @ `org >= 8` | +13.47 | +21.45 | 56.0% |
| bank 50% @ `org >= 5` | +10.22 | +17.71 | 54.6% |

Monotone in tranche size and **win rate falls too**, which the `fs3-00` refutation did not show:
the banked half exits below where the un-laddered exit lands, so the ladder caps the run and
pays an extra leg's fixed cost for it. Same verdict, second geometry.

## The finding that dominates all of the above

The cohort is decaying to death, at `worst_case` on the champion:

| week | n | mean | median | win |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 07-27..08-02 | 67 | +24.72 | +35.56 | 88.1% |
| 08-03..08-09 | 122 | +24.50 | +30.77 | 81.1% |
| 08-10..08-16 | 97 | +12.42 | +27.77 | 74.2% |
| 08-17.. | 5 | **-13.86** | -9.75 | 40.0% |

Launch volume collapses with it — 23 launches on 08-13, then 5 / 8 / 1 / 5. The operator has
largely stopped, and the days he is still active are negative. No exit tuning is worth
anything against that.

## Transferable gates

- **An exit that reacts to the rug is refuted by the lead-time measurement alone.** Measure
  `creator_first_sell - ath_ts` and the ATH-to--50% slot count before searching any exit grid;
  if the first is >= 0 and the second is under ~10 slots, the whole reactive family is dead.
- **Separate an entry *screen* from an entry *wait*.** They fail differently — a wait bleeds
  monotonically through depth, a screen holds per-trade flat and shrinks the book — and only
  the second is evidence that selection is empty rather than merely expensive.
- **Rank a candidate exit against a matched-hold clock**, never against a different hold. Most
  of any exit's apparent edge is the hold length it implies.
- **Check the weekly path before tuning.** Three of the four numbers that matter here were
  available in one group-by.
