# 2026-08-18 — FP108-VET-1 refuted: the +5.95%/trade was a roster leak plus unpriced impact

`FP108-VET-1` (`01e3cc83`, fingerprint `f69c604a` = `3ix:Buy · max=0.108`) was shipped with
`[next_slot_median: +5.95%/trade, OOS +7.60, 93% win]` in its name. Re-derived under a
walk-forward roster and a size-responsive cost model, it does not clear zero.

Rule: entry `m_bundle.veteran_share >= 90` AND `m_flow_window.unique_wallets(1s) <= 9`;
exit `m_position.held >= 120`, `take_profit 10`, `stop_loss 25`.

## What was wrong with the original number

Two errors compounded, both inflating.

1. **Roster leak.** `EngineState::new_track` seeded `BundleState` from the fingerprint's
   *stored* `metric_config`, which the hourly refresher rebuilds from all launches up to
   now. Every historical token in a replay was therefore scored against a roster assembled
   from its own future. Fixed the same day by `RosterTimeline` +
   `veteran_roster::walk_forward_timeline`.
2. **Unpriced own-impact.** The run was priced `pumpfun_fee_only` at a **0.5 SOL** buy.
   `pumpfun_impact` is the only cost model whose charge responds to size, and 0.5 SOL is
   roughly 4x the cost-optimal `sqrt(fixed_per_leg x vsol)` for this cohort.

## Measured

Walk-forward roster throughout (37 veterans at the 08-01 anchor rising to 91 by 08-16).
IS = 08-01..08-09, OOS = 08-09..08-16, both after a 10-day roster warm-up from 07-22.

| buy | fill + cost | window | trades | mean/trade | median | win | PF | total SOL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 | next_slot_median + fee_only | IS | 423 | +2.58% | +8.59% | 88.9% | 1.44 | +5.45 |
| 0.5 | next_slot_median + fee_only | OOS | 90 | +2.84% | +8.37% | 77.8% | 1.68 | +1.28 |
| 0.5 | next_slot_median + **impact** | IS | 423 | **-5.01%** | +3.68% | 87.0% | 0.44 | -10.60 |
| 0.5 | next_slot_median + **impact** | OOS | 90 | **-2.99%** | +3.37% | 70.0% | 0.51 | -1.35 |
| 0.05 | next_slot_median + impact | IS | 423 | +0.53% | +7.23% | 88.2% | 1.08 | +0.11 |
| 0.05 | next_slot_median + impact | OOS | 90 | +1.34% | +6.85% | 77.8% | 1.29 | +0.06 |
| 0.05 | worst_case + default | OOS | 90 | -0.59% | +5.20% | 73.3% | 0.89 | -0.03 |
| 0.02 | next_slot_median + impact | OOS | 90 | +0.40% | +5.86% | 75.6% | 1.08 | +0.01 |

The honest ceiling on the objective is **+0.06 SOL over seven days**. Shrinking size to
escape impact shrinks the edge with it: the per-trade number is bounded above by roughly
+1.3% and the SOL total goes to zero as size does.

## Why no exit config rescues it

Every exit variant tried at the best size returned an identical `worst = -102.2%`:

| variant | mean/trade | PF | total SOL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TP 10 / held 120 / no SL | -0.75% | 0.91 | -0.16 |
| TP 6 / held 30 / no SL | -0.38% | 0.91 | -0.08 |
| TP 6 / held 15 / no SL | -0.51% | 0.86 | -0.11 |
| TP 15 / held 60 / SL 25 | -4.32% | 0.66 | -0.91 |

An unchanged worst trade across four different exit configurations means the exit never
runs: those positions cannot be sold at all. That makes the left tail an **entry**
property, not an exit-tuning problem — `unique_wallets <= 9` selects thin launch bundles,
and thin bundles are exactly the ones with no depth to exit into. A 88% win rate at a
+7% median is fully consumed by a ~6% tail of -66% to -77% trades.

## The selector itself is not what failed

`veteran_share >= 90` still separates: it fires on 423 of 632 matched tokens IS and holds
its per-trade edge out of sample (+2.84% vs +2.58% at zero impact, OOS above IS). What
fails is that the surviving edge is smaller than this cohort's round-trip cost at any size
worth trading. Pair the selector with an entry that does not require selling into a thin
pool before concluding the metric group is worthless.

## Supply collapsed independently

Daily launches for the fingerprint fell from ~80/day (08-01..08-08) to 45, 42, 33, 5, 24,
33, 12, 8 across 08-09..08-16. Even a working rule on this fingerprint has a shrinking
denominator, so re-check the launch rate before spending more on this cohort.

## Incidental fix

`services::veteran_roster`'s creation-slot aggregate read `SUM(amount_lamports)` as `i64`.
Postgres types `SUM(bigint)` as `NUMERIC`, so the decode panicked
(`ColumnDecode { index: "buy_lamports" ... }`) — which killed hunter-live's hourly
refresher task on its first tick. Cast to `::bigint` in the query.
